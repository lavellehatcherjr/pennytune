# Changelog

All notable changes to PennyTune will be documented in this file.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-08-12

The behaviour below is not patch-level: the evidence gate excludes tickers that
previously scored, positive contributors were re-anchored, the solvency cutoffs
moved, and composites for real companies changed.

**Breaking**

- A ticker for which no SEC evidence could be fetched at all is no longer scored or ranked. It is reported as NOT ASSESSED and named on the console, so it is never silently dropped and never ranked against companies whose risk was actually measured. Previously such a name received a positive score and could out-rank a company with real, measured risk findings: in a fifty-name scan, twelve tickers with no SEC identifier at all were scored, every one at an identical value, and they occupied ranks nine through twenty, carrying no flags of any kind.
- Positive quality contributions (valuation, growth, fundamental momentum, financial health) are now graded against fixed published reference bands instead of being ranked against the other tickers in the same run. A run covers at most 100 tickers you choose, so peer groups were routinely a single company: in a fifty-name scan, half the names sat in a peer group of one. Where the underlying metric could be computed at all, the ranking then came out at or near the maximum almost regardless of its value, 9 of 11 for revenue growth and 6 of 7 for fundamental momentum. Composites recorded before this change are not comparable with those produced after it.
- Financial health is now graded continuously from the Altman distress score rather than from its three-value solvency zone, so companies with materially different balance sheets no longer receive identical health credit. The zone previously collapsed roughly 38% of all company pairs into ties, and about 93% of pairs among micro caps.
- The solvency zone cutoffs have been re-calibrated and are no longer Altman's published bands. See **Changed** for the measurement behind this and what it changes.
- Insider credit is withheld when no ownership filings were retrieved at all, instead of paying a company with no insider record a positive contribution for nothing. A company that genuinely files and legitimately scores neutral keeps its credit.
- Exported records gained four columns: which checks could not be run for that company, how many there were, whether its evidence was complete, and its completeness notes. Anything reading the CSV or Parquet by column position rather than by name will need updating, including anything written against the twelve-column schema of the published 0.1.0 release. The column count goes from 12 to 16 and the new columns are inserted before the contribution columns, so every contribution and note column shifts right by four.

**Added**

- Continued-listing trouble disclosed outside 8-K Item 3.01 is now detected, via a full-text search of recent 8-Ks that runs only when no tagged notice was found. Measured against live filings, 19% to 48% of 8-Ks carrying continued-listing language have no Item 3.01 tag, so this was previously missed entirely. It raises a watch-level flag only, never the deficiency tier and never the hard exclusion gate, because the same language appears in filings announcing that compliance was regained.
- Total liabilities are now derived from the balance-sheet identity for companies that never tag them directly, roughly one filer in five and including many large, well-known issuers. This restores distress and financial-health scoring for companies that previously produced a composite with no positive contributions at all.
- Total debt falls back to long-term plus current debt when no combined figure is tagged, roughly halving the rate at which it could not be read.
- Balance-sheet coverage extended to the lease-inclusive debt figure and to the property concepts that post-lease-standard filers, utilities and REITs actually use.
- When an input still cannot be resolved, a single completeness line names exactly which ones are missing, so a partial balance sheet is no longer mistaken for a complete one.
- A check that could not be run is now distinguished from one that ran and found nothing. The ranked table marks how many checks were skipped for each company, the decomposed breakdown labels each uncomputed contributor and lists the checks that never ran, and exports carry all of it.
- A count of unchecked modules alongside the list of them, in every export format. On a real scan the existing boolean is the same value for roughly nine rows in ten, so it sorts and filters almost nothing, while two-unchecked against five-unchecked is actionable. The boolean stays: whether a row is complete at all is a different question from how incomplete it is.
- The README now explains what the composite means, that it is unnormalized and unbounded, that a ticker with no fetched evidence is reported as NOT ASSESSED rather than scored, and what each new export column carries. A Limitations section states the things a reader would otherwise be surprised by: two contributors that are permanently zero, the share of large caps for which the distress score cannot be computed, how often a row rests on partial evidence, that a watched name cannot alert until it has been scanned twice, that comment-letter activity is history rather than an open question, and that there is no cache.
- Translated READMEs in German, Spanish, French, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese and Chinese, with a language switcher at the top of the English README.
- Demo screenshot and thumbnail images in the documentation assets.
- SEC staff comment letters are surfaced as a completeness line: how many letters and how many registrant responses fall in the last 365 days, and the date of the most recent letter. The submissions index records these as form type `UPLOAD` for the staff letter and `CORRESP` for the registrant reply. It does not record what the letter was about, and the staff releases a letter only after the review has closed, so the line is historical by construction and never describes an open question; a letter with no matching response does not mean an unanswered one, because registrants routinely reply inside another filing. Nothing about it is scored. It reaches no composite, threshold or flag, and it costs no extra request, being read from the submissions payload the dilution and insider checks already fetch. Unwindowed the line would say almost nothing: a letter is on file for 84% of micro caps, including 78% of those whose annual report carries no going-concern language. Limited to the last year it appears for roughly a quarter of micro caps.
- 8-K Item 4.02, in which the issuer states that its own previously issued financial statements can no longer be relied upon, is counted and named separately from Item 4.01, a change of auditor. The two were displayed as a single combined figure, so a restatement was indistinguishable from a routine auditor change: one real filer rendered as `4.01/4.02 auditor/restatement×8`, where the eight are six auditor changes and two non-reliance disclosures. Either item still un-gates the dilution detections exactly as before and no composite moves; only the displayed line is split.

**Changed**

- The exact pandas pin became a compatible range, `>=3.0.5,<4`. A fresh install now resolves to whatever 3.x pandas is current rather than to one specific release, so two installs made on different days can differ. The pin was not merely a reproducibility choice: an exact requirement in a published library makes it impossible to install alongside anything that asks for a different pandas, which is a hard failure for the person installing rather than a risk to be weighed. The floor sits at 3.0.5 because 3.0.4 was withdrawn from the index. The committed lockfile remains the reproducible set.
- The mypy pin became `>=2.3,<3` and pydantic gained an upper bound of `<3`. mypy is development-only; the bound on pydantic guards a configuration system built on interfaces that a future major version would be free to change. No such version has been published.
- The lockfile was regenerated. It had drifted from the versions in actual use across thirty packages, so anyone following the documented `uv sync` setup was building against materially older libraries than the test suite runs on, including an older Arrow that writes the export files. Exported column types are unchanged across that move, checked column by column.
- The lockfile was refreshed once more against the current index before release, moving seven further packages. Nothing was added or removed and the resolved set is otherwise unchanged, so the documented `uv sync` setup now installs the same versions the test suite runs on, including the patched releases of two development-only transitive dependencies whose earlier versions carry published availability and timing advisories. Neither reaches an installed copy of the tool: the published wheel declares fourteen runtime dependencies and neither package is among them, at any depth. Exported column types and every composite are unchanged across the move, checked column by column and ticker by ticker.
- The committed lockfile is now watched for updates in its own right. It belongs to a different ecosystem from the dependencies declared in `pyproject.toml`, so routine updates never reached it and it fell behind the ranges it exists to pin. Because the documented contributor setup installs from the lock rather than from those ranges, that gap was what a contributor received.
- The continuous-integration workflow moved to the current major versions of the checkout and Python-setup actions.
- The solvency zone cutoffs moved from Altman's published 1.1 and 2.6 to -3.0 and 1.0. Measured on 194 real filers, using going-concern language in each company's own annual report as an independent distress label: the published boundary at 1.1 sits at the 45th percentile of the real distribution, so it called roughly half the market distressed. It missed none of the 41 going-concern filers, but placed 47 of 153 healthy companies in the distress zone, among them Starbucks, HP, AbbVie, Amgen, Oracle, Lowe's, Duke Energy and AT&T. The re-calibrated cutoffs move those companies out of the distress zone and into the intermediate grey zone, taking the share of healthy filers classified as distressed from 31% to 12%, and still call no going-concern filer safe. Two going-concern filers move from distress to grey. This is a deliberate departure from the published thresholds and is documented as such in the code. The bond-rating-equivalent flags still use Altman's published scale and may therefore disagree with the zone. Note this describes the zone a company is classified into. Until the change described under **Fixed**, the flag shown to the user did not track that classification, and those eight companies still carried a flag reading `distress`.
- The project no longer claims that every flag is traced to the underlying filing. It states that signals are computed from the company's public SEC filings, and names the specific 8-K item for event-driven red flags.
- The translated READMEs carry a short summary of the legal disclaimer pointing to the authoritative English version, rather than a full translated copy.
- README language links use absolute URLs so they resolve on the PyPI project page, not only on GitHub.
- A run may issue one extra SEC full-text request per ticker for the continued-listing check, and only when no tagged Item 3.01 notice was found.

**Fixed**

- The Altman distress score is now withheld rather than reported for companies whose balance sheet falls outside the region the model was built for. A loss-making issuer that had just raised cash and carried almost no liabilities could score as safe on the strength of one unbounded term, with a rounding-sized change in liabilities moving the score by more than the width of the whole middle band. A company that still looks weak once that term is removed keeps its verdict, and profitable companies with earned equity are unaffected. Where the score is withheld the reason is stated, and the company earns no financial-health credit rather than an unfounded one.
- Reverse-split detection no longer reports splits that never happened. The share-count history is now built from the cover-page count, which is not restated after a later split, and one-period collapses too extreme to be a real corporate action are rejected as filer tagging errors. Several large, well-known companies were previously reported as serial reverse splitters, one with a fabricated ratio above 1000-to-1, while genuine serial splitters are still detected.
- The toxic-financing check searches for the exact ownership-cap phrase rather than the individual words, which had been matching essentially any large-cap prospectus. Checks that previously returned dozens of false hits for major issuers now return none, while genuine hits are unchanged.
- `config set` no longer writes a config file that the tool cannot read back. Assigning a plain value to one of the five preset tables, a plausible typo for setting a field inside one, reported success and exited 0 while writing a file that every later run failed to load. The tool was then unusable for scanning, inspecting, reading or changing settings, and re-running setup, with no way to repair it from the command line. The assignment is now refused, naming a field that can be set instead, and nothing is written.
- A config file that cannot be read now reports the path to the file and what is wrong with it, and exits with the configuration-error code. Previously any unreadable config, whether from the bug above, a hand edit, or a file written by a different version, produced an unhandled error and an exit code that scripts are told means results were still written.
- Documentation claims that the code stopped supporting. The project no longer says it covers only major-exchange listings and never OTC, that every signal is computed, that positive sub-scores are sector-and-size percentiles, that trading suspensions are tracked as current versus expired, that the watchlist raises alerts, that `NO_COLOR` is honoured, that responses are cached, or that fails-to-deliver data arrives every two months. Insider filings are documented as being fetched by the project's own hardened client, not by edgartools, which a real run never imports. The first-run risk affirmation asked the user to accept twelve disclaimer sections when there are fourteen. All eight translated READMEs carry the same corrections. A test now fails if the affirmation's section count and the disclaimer disagree.
- An intermediate solvency reading is no longer labelled a distress finding. The solvency penalty fired for both the distress zone and the intermediate grey zone but recorded both under the same name, and that name is in the set that drives the critical red marker. Any company below the safe threshold therefore rendered as a critical distress finding. This was largely invisible until the balance-sheet derivations above made the distress score computable for large filers that previously had none, at which point Starbucks, HP, AbbVie, Amgen, Oracle, Duke Energy, AT&T and Lowe's all began carrying it. Measured on 38 filers against going-concern language in their own annual reports, the critical flag was raised on 14 of 28 healthy companies, against 9 of 28 before the derivations landed, while catching 7 of 10 distressed ones. Every one of those 7 detections came from the distress zone and every grey-zone company in the sample was healthy. The grey zone now reports as `solvency_watch` at caution level, the distress zone keeps `distress` at critical level, and the charge to the score is unchanged for both, so no composite moves. Detection holds at 7 of 10 and the critical flag is now raised on 5 of 28 healthy companies. The grey-zone charge is deliberately kept rather than dropped, because the wider 194-filer sample behind the re-calibrated cutoffs does place 2 of 41 going-concern filers in grey.
- The fiscal year used for analysis can no longer end after the filing that reports it. Forward-looking figures in the notes of an annual report could otherwise anchor the analysis on a date where no balance sheet exists, so every line item came back empty and the company was scored from nothing. This affected roughly 3% of filers.
- The authorized-share headroom flag no longer fires on every company. It was raised by an 8-K Item 5.03 or 5.07 on its own, and because the authorized share count was never populated anywhere in the codebase the measured half could never fire, leaving the event half to decide. Item 5.07 is the annual shareholder meeting, which essentially every issuer files every year, and no recency window applied, so the flag was effectively unconditional: measured across 27 real filers it fired on 27, Apple included, and it moved the dilution severity tier on 18 of them. The authorized count is now read from `us-gaap:CommonStockSharesAuthorized` in the companyfacts payload the dilution provider already fetches, so this costs no extra request and resolved for all 27 filers measured. The flag now fires only when shares outstanding actually reach 90% of the authorized count, and the evidence line states the percentage issued. Item 5.07 no longer contributes at all; Item 5.03 amends the charter and is still recorded, but no longer raises the flag by itself. Where the authorized count cannot be resolved, or where it is reported below shares outstanding because a multi-class or long-stale tagging makes it untrustworthy, the check is suppressed and says so in the completeness notes rather than guessing. After the change 15 of the same 27 filers carry a dilution penalty, and every one of those rests on measured evidence. Composites rise by up to 0.544 for names that carried only the phantom flag.
- `--json scan` no longer skips the export or the exit code. It returned as soon as the payload was printed, so `--json scan --format csv` exited 0 having written no file and given no warning, and a total SEC outage exited 0 while the payload itself reported `"partial_failure": true`, which turns a CI job wrapping the command green on a complete outage. The export now runs and the partial-failure exit code is returned in JSON mode exactly as it is without it. The payload shape is unchanged; the confirmation line for a written export goes to stderr in JSON mode so stdout stays a single parseable document.
- `--format` is validated before anything is fetched, matching `--sort`. The check ran inside the export step after the whole scan had completed, so an invalid format on a live run paid the full SEC round-trip before failing, and when nothing scored it was never reached at all and the command exited 0.
- `--verbose` does something. It was advertised in `--help` as extra diagnostics, and was declared, given an option, and stored, but never read on any path. It now names why each ticker failed to enrich, on stderr. The reasons were already computed and reachable only through the JSON payload, so the plain console reported how many tickers failed but never why, and a total outage was indistinguishable from a mistyped ticker.
- `inspect` strips its argument before resolving it, matching `scan`. A trailing space from a copy-paste resolved to no SEC identifier, so the tool told the user a real company had no filings.
- A config file containing invalid UTF-8 reports the path and exits with the configuration-error code instead of raising a raw traceback. `UnicodeDecodeError` is a `ValueError`, so it escaped the handler that catches the other unreadable-config cases.
- Watchlist alerts fire. They could not, for two independent reasons. The alert set named feature flags (`DELISTING-DEFICIENCY`, `TOXIC-FINANCING`) while a snapshot recorded only scoring-module names (`delisting`, `dilution`), so the two vocabularies had an empty intersection and no new-flag alert could ever match; and the score-drop threshold was 10.0 while the composite spans 5.85 across 27 real filers, so the score alert could not fire either. Even a clean name acquiring every penalty and both hard gates at once produced 13 new flags, a score delta of -3.68, and no alert at all. Snapshots now record all three vocabularies: module names for the human-readable delta, plus the dilution, delisting, halt and manipulation profiles' own flags and the two hard-gate reasons, which is what the alert set matches. Three entries that no code path emits, `DILUTION-HIGH`, `HALTED` and `MANIPULATION-HIGH`, were dropped from that set. The score threshold is now 2.0, chosen above the largest single penalty contribution observed (1.60) so it fires only on compound deterioration the per-flag alerts would not already name, and well inside the reachable range. A watched company that acquires a delisting deficiency now raises `new flag DELISTING-DEFICIENCY` at the top of the next scan.
- Seven local-state failures reported clearly instead of raising a stack trace. An unusable watchlist database, whether corrupt, locked, unwritable, or a directory, and a config file that cannot be written now name the file and exit with the configuration-error code; an export destination that cannot be written names the path and exits with the usage-error code, since the destination is the user's own `output_dir` choice. Every one of them previously produced a raw traceback and exit 1, which the exit-code contract documents as "results are still written and a warning is shown" when nothing had been written. A watchlist that breaks part-way through a scan no longer costs the user the scan: the snapshot write is best-effort, matching the policy already applied when opening the database. Running `scan` with no tickers against a corrupt database reported "the watchlist is empty", which is a different problem with a different fix; it now says the database cannot be read.
- `inspect` has a failure exit path. A nonexistent ticker, a ticker with no SEC identifier, and a total fetch failure all exited 0, so a script could not tell a real breakdown from a lookup that produced nothing. A produced breakdown still exits 0 however heavily suppressed, keeping the NOT ASSESSED distinction intact; a fetch that raised exits with the partial-failure code, the same code `scan` returns for the same ticker under the same fault; a live lookup that produced nothing exits with the usage-error code. `--offline` still exits 0, because no lookup was attempted and `scan --offline` already treats that as success.
- `init` no longer resets tuned weights. Re-running it to change an email address overwrote every weight and penalty and reset `profile = custom` to `hold`, with no warning and exit 0, because the profile fallback was the literal string "hold" rather than the stored profile. It was selective enough to look deliberate: the preset and output format survived the same re-init. The fallback is now the stored profile, so a stored `custom` reaches the guard that preserves user tuning and a stored named profile is simply re-applied. When `--profile` is passed and does change the profile, init now says so and names the reset, matching what `config set profile` already did.
- The `scan` command's help text described a scoring model the code no longer uses. It still said positive sub-scores were sector and size-relative percentiles meaningful only across a large cross-section. That claim was removed from the README and all eight translations when the behaviour changed, but the command docstring was missed, leaving it the only surface in the project still asserting it. It now describes the fixed published anchors that are actually used.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-06-15

Initial public release on PyPI (wheel and source distribution).

**Added**

- `pennytune`, a free command-line forensic due-diligence tool for US-listed micro-caps that surfaces risk signals from a company's SEC filings. It presents evidence rather than verdicts: no buy/sell advice, no predictions.
- Runs entirely on public, no-account, no-API-key data, with SEC EDGAR as the single source and no bring-your-own-key option anywhere. The only identity required is the SEC EDGAR request header (your name and email), stored locally, redacted when settings are displayed, and sent only to the SEC.
- `inspect <TICKER>`, the primary workflow: a full breakdown of one company with its composite score decomposed into contributions.
- `scan`, which ranks a curated set of tickers you name or your saved watchlist, capped at 100 per run. It never scans the whole market and fetches no prices.
- Financial health and distress scoring: Altman Z-double-prime solvency zones plus the Beneish earnings-manipulation and Piotroski strength models over the company's filed financials.
- Dilution and corporate-action risk: shelf and at-the-market offerings, rising share counts and dilution velocity, serial reverse splits, and auditor-change and restatement flags from the 8-K record.
- Insider activity: open-market insider buying kept distinct from routine grants and tax-withholding so awards never read as bullish, plus Form 144 proposed-sale overhang and 13D/13G ownership activity.
- An 8-K material-event tape built from structured item codes and weighted by severity rather than raw count.
- Delisting-notice risk from disclosed continued-listing deficiency notices, reported without guessing a cure-clock day count the tool cannot compute.
- SEC trading-suspension detection, with suspensions recorded within a recency window treated as a hard exclusion.
- Fails-to-deliver settlement-stress context, never treated as evidence of manipulation on its own.
- SIC sector classification for each company.
- A decomposable composite score with user-editable weights, five screening presets (`penny` default, `micro`, `small-cap-value`, `broad`, `custom`) and four strategy profiles (`hold` default, `trader`, `high-return`, `custom`).
- Filter flags including `--exclude-serial-splitter` and `--require-insider-buying`.
- A persistent watchlist (`watch add` / `list` / `rm`) with run-over-run score deltas.
- Export to CSV, Parquet, JSON and Markdown, each carrying a one-line disclaimer header so the disclaimer travels with the data, plus a global `--json` mode.
- Supporting commands: `init` first-run setup (which `scan` and `inspect` refuse to run without), `config get` / `config set`, `sources`, `disclaimer`, `--version` with pinned dependency versions, and `--offline` for a degraded no-network run.
- `scan` output leads with a header showing the active preset and profile alongside data-freshness lines, and ends with the short disclaimer.
- A full legal disclaimer covering the absence of investment advice, the extreme risk of penny stocks, third-party data supplied as-is, non-affiliation with any regulator, and the project's status as an independent personal project.
- MIT licensed, with `NOTICE`, `SECURITY.md` and `CONTRIBUTING.md`. Supports Python 3.11 through 3.14, CI-tested on Linux, macOS and Windows.

**Removed**

These were removed during pre-release development and were never present in any published version:

- GDELT news and coverage analysis. Live runs showed the keyless endpoint was unreliable enough that retries dominated scan runtime, so SEC EDGAR became the single data source and outbound requests were restricted to `sec.gov` only. Sentiment scoring remains in the score structure but is inert, so exports keep a stable shape.
- The `--no-news` scan flag, inert once news was dropped.
- FINRA and nasdaqtrader as data sources, and the Nasdaq screener as the source of the company universe, which now comes from SEC public data.
- The `cache` command and the fetch-caching layer, the DuckDB dependency, and the `--refresh` flag. Nothing in the shipped tool read or wrote a cache.
- Price and universe freshness reporting, which had no live data source. Filing and financials freshness reporting remains.
- The vaderSentiment and FinBERT sentiment dependencies, orphaned when news was removed.
- The PyInstaller standalone-binary distribution. PennyTune ships as a PyPI wheel and source distribution only.

**Dependencies**

- Runtime: pandas, numpy, pyarrow, edgartools, typer, rich, pyfiglet, pydantic, platformdirs, tomli-w, pyrate-limiter, tenacity, defusedxml, requests. Hash-pinned in a committed lockfile; upgrades are reviewed and nothing auto-merges.
